## Takeaway
Coffee Lady is a founder-led, one-name brand (Jan Revill). It trades as a sole trader ("Jan Revill T/a Coffee Lady Consultancy"), not a limited company, and is FCA-registered (FRN 1004670), which lets it arrange equipment leases. It is based around Aylesbury (01844 number) and claims UK-wide coverage. In effect it is a **multi-brand commercial coffee machine dealer, finance arranger and service provider** for offices, hospitality, salons, events and property/facilities managers. It makes money from (1) machine sales at full commercial prices, (2) arranging leases through third-party finance companies, and (3) annual service packages and repairs. It does **not** visibly sell coffee beans or ingredients, offer free-on-loan, publish lease terms, or offer self-serve online ordering for machines (quote-only). Its marketing runs on the founder's personal brand, local networking (she runs a paid business-networking event), a 5.0 Google rating and SEO buying guides.

## How it wins customers
- **Founder brand and networking:** Jan Revill is Regional Lead for Business Buzz Berkshire and hosts the Caversham Buzz (4th Tuesday monthly, £10 + VAT on the door), saying networking "built my business" ([Business Buzz](https://coffeelady.uk/business-buzz/)). That makes the networking event both a lead source and a source of community credibility.
- **SEO content:** blog guides on office machine costs, lease vs buy, best office machines 2026, technician qualifications, error codes, repairs and Franke pricing ([post sitemap](https://coffeelady.uk/post-sitemap.xml)), with a Rank Math SEO plugin and a local-business sitemap.
- **Trust signals:** 5.0★ Google rating, FCA authorisation, authorised-dealer logos, named-founder testimonials.
- **Calls to action:** "Get a Free Quote", "Speak to an Expert", free consultation. No online checkout, instant pricing or trial booking flow.

## Inferences (for the report)
- **Model to copy, not fight head-on:** Coffee Lady proves that a one-person-led brand can sell high-ticket commercial machines without stock, through dealer status, quote-only selling, finance arranged as an FCA-registered broker, and service revenue. Its likely economics are machine margin plus finance commission plus service packages. That is unverified: margins and engineer arrangements are not disclosed.
- **Gaps a newcomer can exploit:**
  1. No coffee or consumables revenue. There is no own-label beans, milk powder or chocolate subscription, so the recurring supply relationship is left to others.
  2. No transparent online pricing for leases, terms, SLAs or callout fees, and figures are inconsistent across pages (£59 vs £84.67/week, 25+ vs 35+ years, 48h vs 1–2 days).
  3. No published trial or "no lock-in" rental product, despite mentioning trials.
  4. Buying terms are seller-friendly: a 50% cancellation fee, a 15% restocking charge and the finance company's lease terms.
  5. The whole brand depends on one person (key-person risk), with no sign of a broader team or digital self-service.
  6. Mostly high-ticket kit (£855–£15k+). There is no clear offer for micro-offices or customer-facing sites at 10–40 cups a day.
- **Partnership or succession angle:** as a sole trader with decades of trading, a full brand-dealer portfolio and FCA authorisation, Coffee Lady could be (a) an installation, service and dealer partner that a newcomer feeds leads to for a fee, or (b) a future customer-book or succession conversation. The owner's intentions are unknown and should not be assumed.
