# Low-Budget Buzz and Inbound Demand for a New UK Coffee Machine and Coffee Business (2023–2026)

Research date: 1 November 2026. Cost and effort estimates in the "Inferences" sections are my own estimates built from the cited rate cards. They are not sourced facts.

## 1. DTC coffee brand case studies: which numbers and tactics drove growth?

### Takeaway
The best-documented low-cost buzz wins came from borrowed audiences, not paid ads: a TV or PR moment (Little's on Dragons' Den: 500%+ in-store uplift and three months of DTC sales in four days), a creator-led crowd event (Hoffmann and Cometeer's roughly 5,000-kit tasting; Hoffmann and Square Mile's roughly 17,500-kit tasting), and swarms of commission-paid micro-creators (Javy on TikTok Shop). Well-funded UK players such as Grind grew through heavy marketing spend while losing money, so they are not a low-budget model.

### Cited Findings
**Little's Coffee (Devon) and Dragons' Den, aired February 2025**
- Turnover was about £4m, up from £3.8m and £3.5m in the two previous years, so growth was close to flat before the show. Steven Bartlett invested £80k for 5% — [BusinessCloud, 11 Feb 2025](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/why-bartletts-dragons-den-gamble-is-already-paying-off/)
- Within 72 hours of airing: in-store uplift of over 500%, three months' worth of DTC sales in four days, email subscribers up 61%, 628,000 Instagram views, No. 1 bestselling instant coffee on Amazon, and stock sold out at Sainsbury's and Waitrose — [BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/why-bartletts-dragons-den-gamble-is-already-paying-off/)
- Its brand positioning rests on the UK's first plastic-free instant coffee packaging and Great Taste awards — [The Dieline](https://thedieline.com/littles-becomes-the-uks-first-plastic-free-instant-coffee/); [Retail Times](https://retailtimes.co.uk/littles-coffee-launches-toffee-nut-flavoured-pods-instant-coffee/)

**James Hoffmann's crowd tastings (creator-led events)**
- 2020 "World's Largest Coffee Tasting" with Square Mile: 2,500 kits were sold by 24 August, before the event — [Barista Magazine](https://baristamagazine.com/james-hoffman-is-planning-the-worlds-largest-coffee-tasting). One blog reports 17,500 kits shipped in total. This is a single source and unverified — [Coffee Magazine](https://magazine.coffee/blog/1/5984/worlds-largest-coffee-tasting-2020-genio-roasters-puts-sa-on-the-map)
- 2023 "Great American Coffee Taste Test" with Cometeer: about 5,000 kits of four frozen coffees were sent out, and about 4,000 survey responses came back. The dataset was later reused publicly as an analytics playground, which extended the reach further — [Maven Analytics](https://mavenanalytics.io/data-playground/great-american-coffee-taste-test)
- Hoffmann also ran a paid decaf tasting project in this format, with kits sold for a home tasting — [Home-Barista forum](https://www.home-barista.com/coffees/james-hoffmann-decaf-tasting-project-now-open-for-orders-t96731-40.html)
- Cometeer's early strategy was to win over the coffee community first, to prove that fast coffee can taste good, and then target mass shoppers. It raised a further $35m in 2021 — [Nosh](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/cometeer-raises-35m-seeks-to-become-the-craft-beer-of-coffee)

**Javy Coffee (TikTok Shop and micro-creators)**
- Analytics-tool estimates (Kalodata and EchoTik) give about $347.7k of US TikTok Shop sales in one month across 8 SKUs, with cold brew at about 64%. Another tool gives a different figure, so treat these as rough — [Baijing](https://www.baijing.cn/article/48977)
- Reported over 7,000 creator videos in 30 days, mostly from small creators, with the top one at 4.6m views. 158 creators ran 402 livestreams in 30 days, and the brand account posts 3–5 videos a week — [Baijing](https://www.baijing.cn/article/48977); [getsaral breakdown](https://www.getsaral.com/academy/javy-coffees-influencer-marketing-breakdown)
- Javy runs an ambassador programme in which creators get unique links and earn commission, and it reshares creator content — [getsaral](https://www.getsaral.com/academy/javy-coffees-influencer-marketing-breakdown)
- Google's case study on Javy's YouTube creator strategy credits the agency Influencer and a "science, synergy, style" framework for choosing creators — [Think with Google UK](https://business.google.com/uk/think/search-and-video/javvy-coffee-youtube-creator-growth-strategy/)
- A job advert states the goal of making Javy the top-selling beverage brand on TikTok Shop — [Rippling job post](https://ats.rippling.com/javvy-coffee-company/jobs/6ee8c53a-ff08-4d53-9027-41d67bb81d1a)

**Death Wish Coffee (free Super Bowl ad through a contest)**
- Won Intuit QuickBooks' "Small Business, Big Game" contest (about 15,000 entries; a public vote across 10 finalists), which gave it a free 30-second Super Bowl 50 spot in February 2016 — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/death-wish-coffee-wins-intuit-contest-for-super-bowl-ad/412964/); [Daily Coffee News](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2016/01/28/death-wish-dream-comes-true-with-super-bowl-ad)
- Super Bowl Sunday sales were reportedly 20–25 times a normal Sunday — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/02/09/making-super-bowl-ad). Intuit's own case study claims a 550% sales rise. That figure is self-reported by the sponsor — [Cannes Lions](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/intuit-quickbooks-small-business-big-game-38280)
- Note: this is from 2016, not 2023–2026. The lesson is to enter sponsor-funded contests.

**Chamberlain Coffee (creator-founder brand)**
- Revenue was about $19m in 2023 and about $22m in 2024 (up 14%), with a loss in 2024. A 2025 projection of $33m (up 53%) is a forecast, not a reported result — [Like & Subscribe / Natalie Jarvey](https://likeandsubscribenews.substack.com/p/exclusive-inside-emma-chamberlains); [Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-emma-chamberlain-investments-the-youtube-star-who-turned-coffee-into-a-33-million-business-by-25)

**Grind (London)**
- Sales rose 38% to about £29.7m in the year to April 2024, split roughly 40% high street, 40% online and 20% grocery and B2B — [World Coffee Portal](https://worldcoffeeportal.com/Latest/News/2025/February/Grind-achieves-record-turnover-as-high-street-outl); [Catering Today](https://www.cateringtoday.co.uk/news/business/grind-coffee-sees-turnover-increase-while-losses-widen/)

In the prior year, £3.8m of marketing spend (promoting the Waitrose partnership and the Bottleshot RTD acquisition) was a key cause of the EBITDA loss — [World Coffee Portal](https://www.worldcoffeeportal.com/news/venues-e-commerce-and-wholesale-yield-strong-2023-sales-for-londons-grind/)
- Crowdfunding as a community tactic: its 2019 Crowdcube round neared £3m from 1,786 investors — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2019/03/145689-success-grinds-latest-crowdcube-round-now-nears-3-million-in-funding/)

**Pact, Rave, Origin and Clevr**
- Pact: most revenue still comes from subscriptions. 99% of customers stayed after a £1-per-bag price rise — [GCR Mag](https://www.gcrmag.com/pact-coffee-ceo-on-bucking-the-subscription-trend). Sales grew only 2% in 2023 after two flat years. The Waitrose listing drove that growth — [World Coffee Portal](https://www.worldcoffeeportal.com/news/pact-coffee-hails-multi-channel-strategy-as-waitrose-partnership-boosts-2023-sales/)
- Rave (Cirencester): turnover rose 22% in 2022 while UK e-commerce fell 10.5%. Coffee Club subscription membership grew over 60% in 2023, which the company tied to customers budgeting monthly. It opened a £1m roastery and café in 2023 — [Punchline Gloucester](https://www.punchline-gloucester.com/articles/aanews/rave-coffee-opens-new-roastery-and-coffee-shop-in-cirencester)
- Origin (Cornwall): the only specialty roaster on the 2023 FEBE Growth 100 list, plus a three-year supply deal with The Hoxton's hotels — [World Coffee Portal](https://www.worldcoffeeportal.com/news/origin-coffee-recognised-as-one-of-the-uks-fastest-growing-private-companies/)
- Clevr Blends started as a travelling pop-up drinks bar and used it for customer feedback. Meghan Markle was its first investor and advocate, and her gifting posts drove awareness — [Shopify blog](https://shopify.in/ca/blog/clevr-blends-traveling-bar-investors); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2021/04/02/clevr-blends-meghan-markle-investment-latte-start-up)
- Black Rifle: built an $80m business by 2019 by selling community and identity to veterans — [Military.com](https://www.military.com/off-duty/2019/12/12/just-how-good-cup-black-rifle-coffee-we-investigate.html)

**Creators and appliance brands**
- Morgan Eckroth (Morgan Drinks Coffee), the 2022 US Barista Champion, was coached by Lance Hedrick (then at Onyx). She has collaborated with Wrecking Ball Coffee Roasters (co-branded coffee) and with Fellow, where she guest-curated "Fellow Drops" — [Sprudge](https://sprudge.com/tiktok-star-morgan-drinks-coffee-launches-collab-with-wrecking-ball-coffee-roasters-172489.html); [HomePage News](https://homepagenews.com/home-housewares/appliances/fellow-morgan-drinks-coffee-team-for-coffee-subscription-program); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Eckroth)
- Sage Appliances UK: in April 2026 it launched the global "Millions of Mornings" campaign around Barista Express owners' rituals, built on user stories — [London Coffee Festival](https://www.londoncoffeefestival.com/journal-1/one-machine-millions-of-mornings). It ran a Mumsnet giveaway of three Barista Express machines (UK only, closing 17 June 2026; the machine retails at £629.95) — [Mumsnet](https://mumsnet.com/articles/win-sage-barista-express). Modash indexes about 50 sponsored creator posts tagged @sageappliancesuk — [Modash](https://www.modash.io/content-library/brands/sage-appliances-examples). WARC holds an earlier social strategy case on Sage's UK launch — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/article/sage_by_heston_blumenthal_bringing_food_thinking_to_the_uk/104813)

### Inferences
- **Crowd tasting kit (copyable).** A "Great British Home Barista Tasting" could ship four blind 30–50g samples in a letterbox box for about £15–£25, priced at cost or with a small margin, and then publish the results as PR and data content. Hoffmann's events show the format works at thousands of units when one trusted creator fronts it. The key spend is a creator partner. Effort: 4–8 weeks to set up.
- **Pitch TV and PR contests.** Dragons' Den, sponsor contests in the style of Intuit's, and Great Taste awards cost little or nothing to enter and can produce step changes. Little's shows that one broadcast moment can bring more than months of DTC sales.
- **Creator-founder brands (Chamberlain) and venture-backed growth (Grind) show that buzz without unit economics brings losses.** Low-budget operators should pair buzz with subscriptions, as Pact's 99% retention after a price rise and Rave's 60% club growth show.
- **For a machine business, the strongest analogue is Sage.** It combines owner stories, community giveaways through Mumsnet, and many mid-size creators, rather than one celebrity.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2023–2026 metrics on Nespresso's marketing (beyond well-known brand campaigns) or on Sage's influencer programme terms, fees or reach.
- No 2025–2026 turnover found for Rave, Origin or Pact. No subscriber numbers or recent revenue for Cometeer or Black Rifle.
- No Hoffmann "Great British Coffee Tasting" UK-specific figures found. The confirmed events are the 2020 global tasting and the 2023 US tasting.
- No verified primary figures for Lance Hedrick's or Morgan Eckroth's commercial deal values.

## 2. TikTok, Instagram and YouTube home-barista trends, creator costs, and TikTok Shop UK

### Takeaway
TikTok Shop UK lets a brand set any non-zero affiliate commission, so creators sell on commission with no upfront fee (apart from free samples). Secondary guides put appliances and electronics at about 5–10% commission, among the lowest categories. Paid UK micro-creators cost roughly £80–£2,000 per post, and UGC-only videos about £50–£250.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok Shop UK: the seller sets the affiliate commission rate. There is no minimum, but 0% is not allowed. Sellers can choose a "Shop plan" with one universal rate or an "Open plan" with custom per-product rates — [TikTok Shop UK seller blog](https://business.tiktokshop.com/uk/blog/detail/10018122)
- Benchmarks: electronics and appliances at about 5–10% (US-focused guide) — [Advertise Purple](https://www.advertisepurple.com/?p=11539). Category range of 2–20%, with electronics lowest — [Optimizon UK](https://optimizon.co.uk/?p=13799)
- UK TikTok Shop platform fees (separate from creator commission) are estimated at 3.8%–9.8% per sale — [EcomCalcTools](https://ecomcalctools.com/blog/fees-tiktok/tiktok-shop-uk/)
- TikTok Shop ads can set different affiliate commission rates for ads — [TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-setting-different-affiliate-commission-rates-for-tiktok-shop-ads?lang=en)
- UK creator rates (2026): nano (500–10k followers) £20–£150 per post and micro £80–£1,500, with TikTok slightly cheaper than Instagram — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/?p=10149). Nano £50–£300 and micro (10k–100k) £200–£2,000 — [Softomate](https://www.softomatesolutions.com/blog/influencer-marketing-uk-businesses-creator-partnerships/). Kolsquare gives UK nano TikTok at about £100–£300 — [Kolsquare](https://kolsquare.com/en/blog/prices-influencers). Awin's older guide gives £19–£95 for micro, which is out of line — [Awin](https://www.awin.com/gb/sector-insights/uk-influencer-marketing-cost)
- UGC videos (the brand posts them) cost about £50–£250 — [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/?p=10149). The US average is about $198 per UGC collaboration, and usage rights can add up to 39% — [Collabstr](https://collabstr.com/blog/influencer-costs-2025)
- Javy's model (thousands of small creators, livestreams and affiliate links) is the clearest coffee example of commission-led TikTok scale — see Section 1 — [Baijing](https://www.baijing.cn/article/48977)
- Morgan Eckroth had about 5.6m TikTok followers and was at roughly 0.8m to 1m YouTube subscribers in conflicting snapshots — [SocialBook](https://socialbook.io/post/top-coffee-creators-on-tiktok-for-brand-partnerships-in-2026)

### Inferences
- **Commission-only TikTok Shop launch.** Set 10–15% on machines (above the appliance norm of about 5–10%, to win creators) and 15–20% on beans and accessories, then seed 20–50 creators with free product. Cost: about £3k–£15k of product at cost if machines are £150–£300 each, plus commission paid only on sales. Effort: about 0.5 FTE for creator outreach and samples, plus 2–4 livestreams a week.
- **Paid starter test.** 10 UK micro-creators at about £300 each (about £3k), or 20 UGC videos at about £150 each (about £3k), to use as ads and product-page content.
- **Content formats that fit coffee machines.** Dialling-in and "fix my shot" content, latte-art progress diaries, ASMR grinding and pouring, "café vs home cost per cup", and machine teardowns. These are inferred from the creator formats above. I found no quantified reach data.

### Gaps
- No TikTok Creative Center hashtag view counts for #coffeetok, #homebarista or #latteart were retrieved. Search returned none, so check them directly in TikTok Creative Center.
- No UK-specific published commission rate for coffee machines on TikTok Shop. Check Seller Center competitors.

## 3. Low-cost buzz tactics: waitlists, trials, pop-ups, quizzes, calculators, SEO and more

### Takeaway
The referral waitlist is the best-documented zero-media-cost launch tactic: Harry's gathered about 100,000 emails in a week, 77% of them via referrals. Pop-up bars (Clevr), free office trials (Hot Numbers), sponsor-funded giveaways (Sage x Mumsnet) and showrooms (Nu Vending) are proven, cheap formats. SEO for "best bean to cup" is dominated by independent review sites such as coffeeblog.co.uk, which suggests getting reviewed by them rather than trying to outrank them early.

### Cited Findings
- Harry's pre-launch: about 100,000 emails in one week, about 77% from referrals. About 20,000 people referred, sending about 65,000 referrals. A two-page microsite offered tiered rewards for climbing the list — [ReferralRock](https://referralrock.com/blog/?p=31149); [Tim Ferriss blog](https://tim.blog/?p=10832). About 15% of the emails were duds, from people gaming the system — [Prefinery](https://www.prefinery.com/blog/referral-programs/prelaunch-campaign/harrys/)
- Clevr Blends grew from a travelling pop-up drinks bar that collected customer feedback before scaling — [Shopify blog](https://shopify.in/ca/blog/clevr-blends-traveling-bar-investors)
- Machine trials (B2B): Hot Numbers Coffee (Cambridge) offers a free two-week office trial with the machine delivered and set up, discounted coffee and no commitment — [Hot Numbers](https://hotnumberscoffee.co.uk/office-coffee). Nestlé Professional runs a machine-trial landing page — [Nestlé Professional](https://www.nestleprofessional.com.sg/coffee-beverages/coffeemachine/trial_officelobby). Nu Vending opened a showroom near Liverpool Street so buyers could "try first" — [The London Economic](https://thelondoneconomic.com/?p=120241)
- Quote and matchmaker tools: Admiral Vending (Wales) launched an AI-powered instant quote tool for UK businesses — [Vending Times](https://www.vendingtimes.com/news/admiral-vending-introduces-ai-powered-coffee-machine-quote-tool-in-uk/)

## 4. B2B buzz: winning SMEs (office coffee)

### Takeaway
UK office-coffee providers lead with free, no-commitment trials, showrooms, instant-quote tools and membership-body offers (such as Chamber of Commerce member deals). I found no documented landlord, coworking or estate-agent referral-fee programmes.

### Cited Findings
- Hot Numbers: free two-week office trial with delivery and setup, discounted coffee, no commitment — [Hot Numbers](https://hotnumberscoffee.co.uk/office-coffee)
- Northern Ireland Chamber member offer: office coffee machines with 500 free drinks and servicing at £75 a month (listing dated June 2025). This shows the chamber and member-benefit channel — [NI Chamber](https://www.northernirelandchamber.com/member-offer/office-coffee-machines-500-free-drinks-and-servicing-75-p-m/)
- Lavazza Pro UK leads with a free, no-obligation consultation across bean-to-cup, capsule and other systems — [Lavazza Pro](https://www.lavazzapro.co.uk/coffee-machines-office/)
- Admiral Vending: AI instant quote tool — [Vending Times](https://www.vendingtimes.com/news/admiral-vending-introduces-ai-powered-coffee-machine-quote-tool-in-uk/)
- Free trials or coffee mornings for local offices are described as a route to long-term leasing contracts — [LocalPage buyer's guide](https://localpage.uk/blog/coffee-machines-buyers-guide-home-office-commercial-options)
- Hospitality B2B anchor deals: Origin with The Hoxton hotels — [World Coffee Portal](https://www.worldcoffeeportal.com/news/origin-coffee-recognised-as-one-of-the-uks-fastest-growing-private-companies/). Grind as Soho House's global coffee partner (2020) — [Grind blog](https://grind.co.uk/blogs/features/international-expansion-brews-at-grind-following-22m-investment-round)

### Inferences
- **Low-cost B2B playbook.** Offer a free two-week trial to offices of 10–50 staff. Use an exclusive Chamber or BID member offer. Pay coworking operators and managing agents a referral fee (for example, one month's rental or about £100–£250 per signed contract; this is an assumption, not a sourced figure). Post on LinkedIn about "coffee as a return-to-office perk" from the founder's account. Cost: a trial fleet of 3–5 machines (about £2k–£6k) plus the founder's time.
- **Coworking spaces double as a showroom.** Placing a machine there at cost turns it into a live demo for hundreds of SMEs.

## 5. Community and loyalty mechanics and their retention results

### Takeaway
Hard, named-brand evidence that Discord or WhatsApp communities cut coffee churn is thin. The best signals are subscription retention after price rises (Pact: 99% stayed), subscription club growth (Rave: over 60% in 2023), and benchmark churn of about 5–10% a month for coffee subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- Pact: 99% of customers stayed after a £1 price rise — [GCR Mag](https://www.gcrmag.com/pact-coffee-ceo-on-bucking-the-subscription-trend)
- Rave: Coffee Club membership up over 60% in 2023 — [Punchline Gloucester](https://www.punchline-gloucester.com/articles/aanews/rave-coffee-opens-new-roastery-and-coffee-shop-in-cirencester)
- Benchmark churn of 5–10% a month is normal for coffee subscriptions — [EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/coffee-subscription-churn-rate-benchmark)
- Peace Coffee (vendor case study): 78% lower day-0 churn and 2–3% monthly churn after subscription tooling changes. This is vendor-reported — [Loop](https://www.loopwork.co/customer-stories/peace-coffee)
- Brûlerie Camélia: churn fell from 9.2% to 6.0% a month, but the vendor says the case is illustrative and anonymised, so it is not verified — [Fullmetrix](https://fullmetrix.com/en/case-studies/abonnement-cafe-retention)
- Super Coffee leaned into subscriptions and actively reduced churn (process case study) — [eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/how-super-coffee-leaned-into-subscriptions-actively-reduced-churn/)
- Black Rifle's community is described as die-hard, and analysts suggest it could monetise community membership. This is speculation — [ProfitWell/Paddle](https://www.profitwell.com/recur/all/boxedout-black-rifle-coffee)
- Grind used Crowdcube to turn 1,786 customers into investors — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2019/03/145689-success-grinds-latest-crowdcube-round-now-nears-3-million-in-funding/)

### Inferences
- **Cheapest mechanics.** A WhatsApp Community or Discord for machine owners (dialling-in help, monthly blind "tasting flights" with a leaderboard), points for reviews and photos, and a crowdfunding round for a capital raise that doubles as customer acquisition. Tools cost about £0–£100 a month. The real cost is about 5–10 hours a week of moderation.
- **Machine plus bean subscription bundles** are the most important retention lever for a machine seller: they turn a one-off hardware sale into recurring revenue.

### Gaps
- No verified named coffee-brand data on Discord or WhatsApp community retention.
- No data on gamified tasting retention.

## 6. Regulatory hurdles (ASA/CAP, CMA Green Claims, DMCC Act 2024)

### Takeaway
Every gifted, discounted, commissioned or paid creator post must carry an upfront "#ad"-style label. Fake or undisclosed incentivised reviews have been automatically unlawful since 6 April 2025. The CMA can fine directly up to 10% of global turnover. The DMCC subscription-contract regime (reminders, easy online cancellation) has been repeatedly delayed, with the latest reports pointing to January or spring 2027. Green claims face supply-chain-wide liability under the CMA's January 2026 guidance.

### Cited Findings
**ASA/CAP influencer rules**
- Each ad needs its own upfront disclosure. Bios do not count. "#ad" is recommended. "#gifted", "#aff" and "affiliate" alone are inadequate. Rules apply to gifts, discounts and commission regardless of follower count. Platform checkboxes alone are insufficient — [Kolsquare UK guide 2026](https://www.kolsquare.com/en-gb/blog/the-complete-guide-to-influencer-post-disclosure-uk-rule); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2025/05/09/asa-publishes-new-report-on-influencer-transparency-102kaqy)
- ASA 2025 monitoring (over 50,000 posts, 390 influencers): about 57% were properly disclosed. Other write-ups say 34% had no disclosure and 9% were poorly disclosed — [Stephens Scown](https://www.stephens-scown.co.uk/intellectual-property-2/brand-protection-2/asa-publishes-its-report-on-influencer-disclosure-compliance-levels-on-instagram-and-tiktok/); [Cosmetics Business](https://www.cosmeticsbusiness.com/influencer-posts-fail-disclose-ties-brands-advertyising-standards-authority)
- February 2026 ASA research found 70% of people want ad labels visible upfront — [Retail Gazette](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/02/influencer-marketing-less-recognised-than-brand-ads-says-asa-research/)
- The CMA and ASA updated their joint influencer guidance for the DMCC Act (fourth edition) — [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2025/cma-and-asa-publish-updated-influencer-guidance-on-social-media-endorsements/); [LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/news/asa-cap-publish-updated-influencer-advertising-guidance-following-dmcca-2024-changes)
- The brand is liable as well as the creator. Brands share responsibility for disclosure — [Kolsquare](https://www.kolsquare.com/en-gb/blog/the-complete-guide-to-influencer-post-disclosure-uk-rule)

**DMCC Act: fake reviews and enforcement**
- Since 6 April 2025, it is automatically unlawful to write, commission or host fake reviews, or to present incentivised reviews without disclosing the incentive. Misleading presentation, such as giving prominence to positive reviews or moving reviews between products, is also banned — [Hill Dickinson](https://www.hilldickinson.com/our-view/articles/digital-markets-competition-and-consumers-act-2024-new-consumer-law-protections-now-in-force/); [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/fake-reviews-and-sneaky-fees-banned-under-new-law-campaigned-for-by-which-aLzVp4g0S76D)
- The CMA can directly fine up to 10% of global turnover or £300,000, whichever is greater — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/insights/legal/new-consumer-enforcement-regime.html)

**DMCC subscription contracts**
- The regime covers pre-contract information, renewal reminders, easy cancellation (online if bought online) and cooling-off rights — [LegalVision](https://legalvision.co.uk/commercial-contracts/dmcc-act/)
- Timing conflicts. It was delayed from spring 2026 to autumn 2026 and then to spring 2027 — [Lewis Silkin, Apr 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/04/02/consumer-law-update-subscriptions-regime-delayed-again-to-spring-2027-102mops); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/dmcc-subscription-rules-delayed-to-spring-2027-key-takeaways-from-the-consultation-response/). Later reports say it was brought forward to January 2027 — [Brodies](https://brodies.com/insights/commercial-contracts-and-outsourcing/the-dmcca-subscription-contracts-the-timeline-just-got-shorter/); [Acquired](https://www.acquired.com/blog/dmcca-subscription-legislation-january-2027/). Check legislation.gov.uk.

**CMA Green Claims Code**
- The 2021 Code is guidance, and claims must be truthful, clear, substantiated, consider the full life cycle, and so on. Since April 2025 the CMA has been able to fine directly under the DMCC Act — [Lewis Silkin](https://lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/01/19/get-dmcc-ready-dont-forget-about-sustainability-102judw); [Weil](https://antitrust.weil.com/giving-the-green-light-new-uk-direct-enforcement-powers-to-tackle-green-claims/)
- 22 January 2026 CMA supply-chain guidance: manufacturers, distributors and retailers can each be liable for green claims, not only the brand owner — [Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/food-for-thought-spring-2026/cma-green-claims-supply-chain-guidance); [Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/uk-competition-and-market-authority-provides-new-green-marketing-guidance-for-all-supply-chain-actors/)

### Inferences
- **Practical rules for the tactics above:**
  - Waitlist and referral rewards must not buy reviews. Any review from a customer who got free product must be labelled "incentivised".
  - TikTok Shop affiliate creators must use "#ad" even when paid only by commission.
  - Cost-per-cup calculators need documented assumptions.
  - Claims such as "compostable pods", "carbon neutral" or "eco trade-in" need life-cycle evidence.
  - Machine and bean subscriptions should already offer easy online cancellation and renewal reminders, to be ready for the 2027 regime.
- **Compliance cost** is mostly process: a creator brief template, a review-moderation policy and a claims evidence file. That is roughly 1–3 days of setup plus an optional £500–£2k legal review (my estimate).
